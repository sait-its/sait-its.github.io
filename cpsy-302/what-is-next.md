### The Internet Runs on Linux

- This course focused heavily on Windows Server. Although we worked with Linux along the way, time constraints meant we only scratched the surface.
- Much of the cloud infrastructure, web services, and container platforms we rely on every day are built on Linux.
- If you're interested in cloud computing, DevOps, cybersecurity, or infrastructure administration, I strongly encourage you to keep developing your Linux skills.

---

### A Few Areas Worth Exploring

- **Linux Storage:** Partitions, modern filesystems (XFS, btrfs, ZFS), LVM, and storage management.
- **Linux Networking:** Network interfaces, routing, firewalls, bridges, VLANs, and network namespaces.
- **Container Primitives:** Learn how Linux namespaces, cgroups, and overlay filesystems make containers possible. Go beyond running Docker commands and understand what's happening underneath.
- **Automation:** Get comfortable with Bash scripting, Ansible, and Infrastructure as Code.

---

### A Few Words About AI

- AI is changing how we work and learn, and I encourage you to embrace it.
- But please don't treat AI as a machine that simply spits out answers for you to copy and paste.
- Use it as a tutor. Ask it to explain concepts, challenge your understanding, help you troubleshoot, and explore different approaches.
- Most importantly, **always verify what AI tells you.** AI can sound very confident while being completely wrong. Understanding *why* something works is far more valuable than getting it to work by blindly following instructions.

---

### Don't Stop Here

- You don't need expensive equipment or a fancy home lab to keep learning. A few VMs, some curiosity, and a willingness to break things and fix them can take you a long way.
- Being good at IT isn't about memorizing commands or knowing all the answers. It's about knowing how to learn, how to investigate problems, and how to find solutions. Technologies will change, tools will come and go, but those skills will stay with you.
- Keep building things, keep breaking things, keep asking questions, and most importantly, don't stop learning just because the course is over.
